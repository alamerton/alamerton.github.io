### TL;DR
I created three blog posts trying to connect the policy actions from week 6 with technical implementations. I created short blog posts with visuals in an iterative fashion, focusing more on creating content rapidly and learning from iteration than the quality of the individual posts, and now feel more confident in my ability to communicate using this format, and connecting high-level goals to pragmatic implementations.
### What I Achieved 
An important element of this project is that I wanted to use it to encourage and promote myself into doing more stuff like this in general. I wanted to use the project as a mechanism to put myself into the habits of generating permissionless content rapidly and improving it in iterations via feedback. I believe it has successfully served this purpose, and I am proud of the improvements I have made over the project sprint.

It was also an exercise in taking a policy goal that is somewhat unspecific by nature (which is natural, and to be expected to some degree), and imagine from first principles what an implementation might look like. Unsurprisingly, I did not find this as easy as expected, but I do believe that I achieved this to some degree. I want to improve the skill of designing a practical implementation of a goal, and this project helped me train that muscle.

One of the main pressures on this project was time. Due to my circumstances I have only been able to afford ~1.5 hours of work per week on the project, meaning I have not been able to pay the depth of attention necessary to make my content remarkable. I have, however, gotten the chance to experiment with working with ideas, rapidly building and publishing content, and graphic design. I can honestly say that despite not spending much time on the content, I do feel like the practice has been deliberate and I have improved through iteration.

### Some Things I Learned
1. I can't rush research. Despite trying to create short posts with specific content, the limitations in the quality of my research are evident. I am now more of the belief that good quality takes time.
2. Practice is helpful for me. I saw my graphic design abilities improve over just a few iterations, mostly because I allowed myself to make mistakes and not overthink each creation. This let me focus on not making the same mistakes again and expanding on the things I liked about my diagrams.
3. I now think it's a good idea to create explainers using colourblind-friendly colours. At first I struggled to find a palette I liked, and I'm still not exactly sure how to make a diagram that people with any class of colourblindness can see (it might have to be greyscale!), but I enjoyed exploring this. Shout out to Dana Bates for recommending this to me!
### What Did Not Go Well
As I was restricted on time, much of my writing is incomplete. This is due to the short amount of time I gave to the project overall, and the fact that my time was also spent creating diagrams, exploring platforms, and split across creating multiple posts.

I did not seek as much feedback on this project as I could have. If I had continued to seek feedback after the sessions ended, I may have ended up with more concise posts and better writing. This is once again due to my time restriction. However, the overall quality of my content was not the only goal of this project for me, so it is not a significant problem.

Lastly, the quality of the blog posts also suffers from a lack of citations. While I was mostly trying to reason about the policy actions from first principles, some of the content could definitely do with references (especially, for instance, the deepfake post, where I pivot more to a report on the state of the research).

### What I Will Do Next
I plan to continue creating blog posts, so I thought I would add this 'further work'-like section to reflect on my project's flaws, and plan the next steps.
1. Spend more time conducting research before starting to write, so I have references, diagram ideas, and notes all ready to weave together, rather than hacking it together on the fly
2. Migrate the content to a new platform. GitHub pages is not an ideal medium for my goals here, I will start by migrating the posts to Substack, before attaching them to a branch of my personal website (which does not exist yet)
3. Refactor/finish existing posts. I may not decide to finish all of the posts, or I may decide to refactor them into a new post. But either way, I would like them to look more finished than they currently do. Once I do this I can cross-post them to platforms with more reach (e.g., the EA forum or LessWrong)

In conclusion, this project has been a very useful exercise for me. I feel ready to continue thinking about AI governance, and publish my thoughts and findings publicly. I now feel like I have the tools to be a better online communicator, and am excited to continue this process.
